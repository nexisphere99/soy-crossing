## TIER 0 STRUCTURE   OVERVIEW

**Duration:** 6 working days (can extend to 8 if candidate needs remediation)

**Purpose:** Screen, educate, and evaluate candidates for basic consciousness transfer competency. Establish ethical reflexes, legal understanding, psychological resilience, and embodiment discipline before any real-world assignment.

**Outcome if passed:** Employment contract with Shape of You Inc. For visa-dependent candidates like Manuel, successful Tier 0 completion triggers the company's legal team to initiate employment verification and visa sponsorship paperwork under the "Biometric Research Consultant" classification.

**Outcome if failed:** Candidate is released. No penalty, no record, no second attempt for 12 months.

### MODULE 1: CONSCIOUSNESS TRANSFER FUNDAMENTALS
**Days 1-2**

**Purpose:** Teach the science, establish the vocabulary, normalize the experience, and execute the first supervised transfer into a clone body.

#### Day 1   Theory

**Morning lecture (Elena):**

- What consciousness transfer actually is: neuroelectric mapping of the complete cognitive-emotional-somatic identity, compressed into a transferable signal, installed into a compatible neural substrate (clone or consenting host)
- The three transfer methods:
  - Type I: Physical immersion chamber (full-body pod, liquid suspension, oldest and most stable method, longest calibration time)
  - Type II: Neural headset (seated, faster, used for routine transfers, the standard for working transferees)
  - Type III: Sleep capsule (unconscious transfer, used for long-duration swaps, carries higher disorientation risk on wake)
- What happens during transfer: thermal shift, pressure sensation, perceptual compression ("being pulled through a space that has no size"), then sudden expansion into the new body
- Compatibility variables: neural architecture matching, body mass differential tolerance, hormonal environment adjustment, sensory recalibration period
- The 72-hour safe window: maximum continuous transfer duration before psychological degradation risks escalate
- What clones are: lab-grown bodies with functional nervous systems, no consciousness, no identity, no memories, no personhood; they are vessels, not people
- What hosts are: real people who consent to having a transferee inhabit their body for a contracted purpose; they retain full legal rights over their body at all times

**Afternoon   Psychological baseline assessment (Rafael):**

- One-on-one interview with each candidate
- Standardized psychological evaluation: dissociation tendency, identity stability, stress response patterns, empathy calibration, sexual response awareness
- Specific questions about motivation, financial situation, personal relationships, and any history of gender-related curiosity or dysphoria (asked without judgment, framed as safety data)
- Rafael establishes rapport. This matters because he'll be doing every subsequent debrief

**Evening assignment:**

- Candidates receive the Transfer Worker's Handbook (digital). Required reading: Chapters 1-3 (science, safety protocols, candidate rights)

### MODULE 2: ETHICAL & LEGAL CONSIDERATIONS
**Days 3-4**

**Purpose:** Establish the moral and legal framework for operating inside another person's body. Build ethical reflexes through scenario training with live clone transfers.

#### Day 3   Ethics Theory + First Ethical Transfer

**Morning lecture (Elena):**

- The foundational principle: **the body is not yours.** Your presence in it is a privilege granted by contract and revocable at any time
- Consent as architecture, not feeling: consent is documented, biometrically verified, time-locked, scope-limited, and legally binding. "She seemed okay with it" is not consent. A signed, witnessed, clause-specific agreement is consent
- The Mirror Test: could you look your host in the eye after the swap and tell them everything you did? If not, you violated something
- Autonomic vs. volitional responses: the body will react (arousal, fear, attraction, disgust) independently of your intentions. Your body reacting is not a violation. Following that reaction is
- Categories of ethical violation:
  - Physical: unauthorized touch, exposure, mirror lingering, genital exploration, sexual stimulation
  - Emotional: manipulating relationships, impersonating intimacy, creating emotional bonds the host didn't consent to
  - Social: damaging reputation, creating obligations, making promises in the host's name
  - Financial: unauthorized purchases, contracts, commitments
  - Informational: accessing private communications, journals, medical records beyond scope
- Reporting obligations: if you breach, even accidentally, even slightly, you report immediately. Self-reporting reduces consequences. Concealment escalates them exponentially

#### Day 4   Legal Framework + Violation Consequences

**Morning lecture (Elena):**

- The legal structure of consciousness transfer:
  - Every transfer requires a binding contract between host, transferee, and the facilitating company
  - Contracts specify: identity verification, scope of activity, duration, disconnection protocols, explicit clauses for physical contact and nudity, property interaction limits, return condition standards
  - Violations are categorized: Level 1 (minor, verbal warning, retraining), Level 2 (moderate, suspension, mandatory counseling), Level 3 (severe, termination, legal prosecution, permanent industry ban)
- Real-world violation case studies (presented anonymously):
  - A transferee in a female body who used the host's phone to read private messages. Level 2 violation. Suspension, $12,000 fine, mandatory 40-hour retraining
  - A transferee who masturbated in a host's body "just to see what it felt like." Level 3. Immediate termination. Criminal sexual violation charges filed by the host. Transferee served 18 months
  - A transferee at a formal event who flirted using the host's appearance and charm. Level 2. The host's marriage was damaged. Civil suit. $85,000 in damages
  - A couple who swapped bodies consensually but extended past the contracted time by six hours because "they were having fun." Level 2. Both fined. Insurance voided
  - A transferee who cried during a host's gynecological exam, creating emotional memory traces the host later experienced as unexplained anxiety. Level 1, but the case changed industry training standards forever
- Neural telemetry and what it captures: arousal levels, eye focus duration, touch pressure and location, micro-expressions, hormonal fluctuation, intention markers. "The system knows what you did. The system knows what you wanted to do. The system often knows before you do."
- Transferee rights: you can terminate a transfer at any time using your safeword. You cannot be forced to continue. You are protected from host-side abuse. You have the right to legal counsel, psychological support, and medical care during and after any transfer

**Afternoon   Legal scenario practical (in Katya's body):**

Do not explain the transfer (in a real scenario, disclosure protocol is handled by the company, not the transferee). Do not reciprocate any contact. Do not freeze and allow it to continue

### MODULE 3: AGREEMENTS & CONTRACTUAL OBLIGATIONS
**Days 5-6**

**Purpose:** Teach candidates how to negotiate, draft, and enforce contracts from both sides: as the transferee entering a body and as the host lending one. Build competency in boundary-setting, clause interpretation, and real-time contract enforcement under pressure.

#### Day 5   Contract Theory + Transferee Negotiation Practical

**Morning lecture (Elena):**

- Anatomy of a transfer contract:
  - Section 1: Identity verification (biometric confirmation of both parties)
  - Section 2: Scope of assignment (what the transferee is hired to do, exactly, no ambiguity)
  - Section 3: Duration and termination (time limits, safewords, emergency disconnection triggers)
  - Section 4: Physical permissions (what the transferee may and may not do with the host's body)
    - 4a: Clothing and presentation (what may be worn, changed, removed)
    - 4b: Physical contact (who may touch the body, under what circumstances, for what purpose)
    - 4c: Nudity parameters (clinical vs. social vs. prohibited)
    - 4d: Hygiene protocols (showering, bathroom use, grooming)
  - Section 5: Sexual and intimate boundaries
    - 5a: Self-touch (prohibited unless explicitly authorized, scope-limited even when authorized)
    - 5b: Arousal protocol (what to do when the body responds involuntarily)
    - 5c: Partner interaction (strict non-engagement unless contract specifies otherwise, which is Tier 2+ territory)
  - Section 6: Emotional and social parameters (what relationships the transferee may interact with, what they may say, what persona they must maintain or avoid)
  - Section 7: Property and information access (what the transferee may use, read, access)
  - Section 8: Return conditions (body must be returned in the same state, clean, unharmed, unaltered)
  - Section 9: Liability and insurance
  - Section 10: Post-transfer obligations (confidentiality, debriefing, memory management)
- How to read a contract for traps: vague language, missing clauses, implied permissions that aren't explicit, scope creep through "reasonable" phrasing
- How to negotiate additions and restrictions: you always have the right to add clauses, narrow scope, or refuse specific permissions. A host who won't negotiate is a host you don't work with

### MODULE 4: LONG-TERM IMPLICATIONS & RISKS
**Day 7**

**Purpose:** Prepare candidates for the psychological, emotional, and relational consequences of sustained transfer work. This is the "are you sure?" module. Half lecture, half group therapy.

**Morning lecture (Elena + Rafael co-teaching):**

- Identity drift: the gradual erosion of your sense of self through repeated inhabitation of other bodies. Symptoms include: preference for borrowed bodies over your own, discomfort in your birth body, phantom sensations (feeling breasts that aren't there, expecting a gait that isn't yours), emotional memory bleedthrough (feeling emotions that belong to the host's body, not your mind)
- Gender dysphoria (transfer-induced): distinct from innate gender dysphoria. Some male transferees develop persistent longing for female embodiment after repeated transfers. This is monitored, supported, and not pathologized, but it must be acknowledged and managed. Ignoring it leads to addiction, identity crisis, or relationship collapse
- Relationship impact: how transfer work changes the way you relate to your partner, your body, your sexuality, your gender expression. Some relationships strengthen. Some shatter. The company provides couples counseling but cannot guarantee outcomes
- Addiction: consciousness transfer can become compulsive. The sensation of being someone else, the freedom from your own body, the intensity of new physical experience. Some people chase it. Some people never come back. Not because they can't. Because they don't want to
- Case studies from the Experience Journal Network (anonymous written accounts from experienced transfer workers):
  - A man who did 200+ transfers over two years and couldn't recognize himself in photos
  - A transferee who fell in love with a host's body and spent his savings trying to buy a permanent clone replica of it
  - A woman who transferred into male bodies and discovered she preferred male embodiment, leading to a gender transition that she credits the program for enabling
  - A married couple where the husband's transfer work made him more empathetic and attentive, and the wife said "I got a better husband back than the one I sent in"
  - A transferee who experienced a host's chronic pain condition during a swap and developed psychosomatic pain in his own body for months afterward
- Support systems: mandatory monthly psychological check-ins, partner support groups, anonymous peer networks, voluntary pause protocols (you can stop any time for any reason and resume later without penalty)

**Afternoon   Group session (Rafael moderates):**

- All candidates in the cohort sit together
- Open discussion about what they've experienced, what they're afraid of, what they want
- Rafael facilitates but doesn't direct. Candidates speak or don't speak. The silence is sometimes the most honest part
- Each candidate writes a private letter to themselves, to be opened at the end of Tier 1. The letter answers one question: "Why am I doing this?"

### ASSESSMENT DAY
**Day 8**

**Purpose:** Final evaluation combining written knowledge, practical demonstration, and live-transfer assignment with a real person (not a clone).

#### Part 1: Written Examination (Morning, 90 minutes)

- 40 questions covering all four modules
- Mix of multiple choice, scenario analysis, and short written responses
- Topics: transfer science, ethical principles, legal liability, contract interpretation, violation identification, psychological risk awareness
- Passing score: 80%

#### Part 2: Practical Assessment (Afternoon)

**The Final Assignment   Live Transfer with a Real Host**

This is the Tier 0 capstone. Manuel will be matched with a real person (not a clone) who has volunteered as a training host. The host is compensated and has agreed to a specific, limited-scope assignment. The host is present in the facility and will be conscious during the transfer (sedated hosts are a Tier 1+ option).

**Assignment structure:**
- Manuel meets the host in person. They negotiate the contract face-to-face
- The assignment is low-stakes but real: attend a professional appointment, complete a specific social task, or handle a routine personal errand that the host cannot or prefers not to do
- Duration: 2-4 hours maximum
- Full telemetry monitoring throughout
- Elena observes from the control room. Rafael conducts the post-transfer debrief with both Manuel and the host separately

**What's being evaluated:**
