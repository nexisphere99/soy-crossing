## OPTIONAL: NEIGHBORHOOD WALK

### Passage: `t0_day04_walk_neighborhood`

I take the long way home. Not because I want exercise. Because I need to feel my own body moving through space in a straight line without hip sway, without breasts shifting, without skin that registers every temperature change like a weather station.

Ninth Street to Seventh. Past the laundromat that always smells like bleach and Mexican radio. Past the Dominican barbershop where three guys are sitting in chairs that aren't barber chairs, just chairs, drinking from paper bag bottles and watching the street like it's television.

One of them nods at me. I nod back. The exchange takes less than a second and contains an entire conversation: *I see you. You see me. We are men on this street. We have acknowledged each other's existence. That is sufficient.*

I couldn't have described that transaction a week ago. Now I can. Now I know it's a thing men do that women don't get to do, this wordless mutual acknowledgment that costs nothing and means nothing and is invisible until you've stood on the other side of it in a body that doesn't receive it.

I pass Lulu's bodega. The OPEN sign is on. I can see her through the window, restocking cans on the middle shelf, standing on a step stool because she's five-two and the shelf is five-seven. I could go in. She'd feed me. She'd interrogate me. She'd mention the bartender with the tattoos.

I keep walking.

Two blocks further. The park on Sixth. Small, scrubby, more concrete than grass, but there's a bench by the basketball court and nobody's playing right now. I sit down.

The bench is cold through my jeans. The sky is doing that October thing where the blue gets diluted, washed out, like someone added too much water to the paint. A woman jogs past on the path. Ponytail swinging. She adjusts her earbuds without breaking stride and I notice the way her sports bra compresses her chest and the way she runs with her elbows tight, taking up minimum lateral space, and I think about Katya's body on the couch with its legs tucked under, compact, nested, making room.

The woman is making room too. Even while running. Even while alone. The body negotiates with space even when nobody's watching.

I sit on the bench for twenty minutes. The cold seeps through the denim into my thighs. I let it. The sensation is specific to this body, to these thighs, to jeans and cold metal and a flat chest that doesn't shift when I lean forward to put my elbows on my knees.

My body. Specific. Not default. One version.

I get up and walk home.

## MAIN SIDE QUEST: JAMES DINNER (SQ-003)

### Passage: `t0_day04_james_dinner`

Siam Corner is on a side street I've never walked down. Small, bright, twenty tables crammed so close the chairs overlap. The walls are covered in framed photos of what I assume is the owner's family, a dynasty of cooks grinning over pots and woks across what looks like four decades. The air is thick with lemongrass and chili oil and the sizzle of something hitting a wok at exactly the right temperature.

James is already there. Of course he is. James is always early. It's the guilt engine that runs him, the perpetual awareness that he survived the layoff and I didn't, translated into small acts of punctuality and generosity that he'll never admit are penance.

He's at a two-top by the window. He stands up when he sees me and his face does that thing, the James thing, where relief and warmth and a little bit of shame all compete for the same expression. He pulls me into a hug. James hugs like he means it. Both arms, full contact, the kind of hug that says *I am glad you exist and I feel bad about the world.*

"Man." He steps back. "You look different."

"Different how?"

"I don't know. Tired but, like, focused? Like you've been doing something intense." He sits. "The menu's incredible. I already ordered spring rolls. Don't argue."

"I wasn't going to argue."

"You always argue." He pushes a menu across the table. "Get the pad thai. My coworker swears on her grandmother's grave."

A Tupperware container appears from his bag. Blue lid, fogged with condensation. "Mom's chicken casserole. She put extra cheese because, and I quote, 'that boy needs comfort food.'"

"She's never met me."

"She's seen photos. She says you have 'kind eyes and a hungry face.'" He pushes the container toward me. "Take it. If I bring it back she'll assume you're dead and start a prayer chain at church."

I take the casserole. It weighs about two pounds. Mrs. Okafor does not mess around.

### Passage: `t0_day04_james_dinner_02`

The spring rolls arrive. They're perfect. Crispy, not greasy, with a peanut sauce that has actual peanut chunks in it. James eats one in two bites and starts talking about his biotech side project with the specific enthusiasm of a man who has been waiting all day for someone to tell.

"So the genome sequencing thing, right? I figured out the bottleneck. It was in the data pipeline, not the sequencing protocol. The parser was reading every strand as a discrete object instead of mapping relational clusters. I rebuilt the parser in Rust and the throughput tripled."

"Tripled?"

"Tripled." He grins. "My advisor said it was 'promising.' From Dr. Chen, that's basically a marriage proposal."

I eat a spring roll and let his energy fill the table. James talks with his hands. He gestures so broadly that he almost knocks over the water glass twice. He has the specific quality of a man whose brain runs faster than his mouth and his mouth runs faster than most people's brains. Keeping up with James is like jogging beside a sprinter who doesn't know he's sprinting.

The pad thai arrives. It's transcendent. James's coworker was right.

"So." James puts his chopsticks down. Gets serious. The shift is visible, his posture changes, shoulders coming in, voice dropping half a register. "The new job. How's it going? For real."

"It's intense." I eat a noodle. Chew. Buy time. "Good, though. Different from anything I've done."

"Different how?"

The question sits between us like something breakable. I pick it up carefully.

"It's physical. Not like construction. More like..." I reach for words that are true without being complete. "Learning how different bodies work. From the inside."

James tilts his head. "Like physical therapy?"

"Something like that." The lie is small and round and it slides between my teeth with the ease of practice. Four days of practice. "The company does research on how people experience physical sensation. Biometric stuff. I'm a consultant."

"Biometric research consultant." James says it slowly, tasting the words. "That's the most Manuel sentence I've ever heard. Vague, technical, and slightly evasive."

"I can't talk about specifics. NDA."

"Right." He nods. He doesn't push. James never pushes. He's built his entire personality around giving people room, which is what happens when you grow up with four siblings and a mother who ran the household like a benevolent general.

"But you're okay? Like, physically? It's safe?"

"Very safe. Monitored. Supervised. Medical team on site."

"And the visa? This helps?"

"Diana thinks they can file a transfer petition. If I pass the certification."

"Manuel." He leans forward. "That's amazing. That's, like, actually amazing." His face is doing the James thing again, the relief and the warmth, and this time the shame is smaller. "You deserve this. You know that, right? After everything with Brevity and the job search and all of it. You deserve something that works."

"Thanks, James."

"I mean it. You're the hardest-working person I know, and I work with a guy who sleeps under his desk." He picks up his chopsticks. "Now eat your noodles. They're getting cold."

I eat my noodles. They're still transcendent.

### Passage: `t0_day04_james_dinner_03`

The bill comes and James's hand shoots across the table like a cobra striking a mouse.

"James."

"Don't."

"I can pay for my own food."

"My treat." He's already putting his card down. "When you're a certified biometric research whatever and making bank, you can buy me dinner. Until then, let me have this."

I let him have it. The guilt is consistent if nothing else.

We walk out into the cold. The restaurant's warmth clings to our clothes for about ten seconds before October strips it away. James pulls his collar up. He's wearing a puffy jacket that makes him look like a friendly marshmallow.

"Thursday dinner number, what, twelve?" he says.

"Something like that."

"I'm glad we do this. I know it started because, you know." He waves his hand. The guilt. The layoff. The survivor's arithmetic. "But it's not about that anymore. For me. I hope you know that."

"I know."

He claps my shoulder. "Get home safe. Eat the casserole. Text me when you do or my mom will call."

He turns toward his bus stop. I watch him walk, this big warm generous man who feels guilty about keeping his job and pays for it in pad thai and chicken casserole and Thursday nights, and I think about the version of honesty I can't give him yet. The body swapping. The female bodies. The hand on the knee. Someday I'll tell him and he'll process it the way James processes everything, with wide eyes and too many questions and eventually a hug.

Someday. Not tonight.

## PACO CALL (SQ-010 + Main debrief)

### Passage: `t0_day04_paco_call`

I call Paco from the walk home. He picks up on the second ring.

"Hermano! How was legal day? Did they show you the prison one?"

"They showed me the prison one."

"Eighteen months." Paco whistles. "For jerking off. In someone else's body. I mean, the audacity. The AUDACITY."

"Paco."

"I'm not saying I condone it. I'm saying if you're going to commit a crime, at least commit an interesting one. That guy got eighteen months for being boring AND horny."

I laugh. It hurts a little, in the ribs, from the dinner and the spring rolls and the day. "That's not the point."

"What's the point?"

"The point is the scenario."

"Which scenario?"

"Scenario 4. The Partner."

Silence. Paco's silence is notable because it happens so rarely. "Tell me."

I tell him. The living room. The couch. The actor coming through the door. *Hey, Katya? Missed you today.* The hand on the knee.

"And what happened?" Paco's voice has shifted. Curious, careful, the way he sounds when he knows he's about to hear something that will change the shape of what he understands.

"The body reacted. Before I could think. Heart rate doubled. Skin flushed. Everything, like, opened. Toward him. The body knew him and the body wanted him and none of that was mine. None of it was my choice."

"But it felt like yours."

"It felt like being inside a choice someone else already made."

"Okay." Paco breathes. "So you were in a woman's body and a hot guy touched your leg and your body wanted to fuck him."

"That's an extreme simplification."

"But accurate."

"The body responded. I didn't choose it."

"Right. But what was that like?"

I walk past a bodega, not Lulu's, a different one. The neon signs reflect off the wet sidewalk, red and green smeared on asphalt. "Confusing. Like being a passenger in a car that's accelerating and you can't find the brake."

"Did you stop it?"

"I stopped it. Put my hand over his. Moved it. Said I needed a minute."

"In her voice?"

"In her voice."

Paco is quiet for three beats. I can hear background noise on his end, television maybe, the low murmur of a Spanish broadcast.

"Manuel."

"Yeah."

"Are you okay?"

The question is different from James's. James asked about safety, logistics, the visa. Paco is asking about the thing underneath the safety. The thing that has no name yet.

"I don't know. Yeah. I think so. It's just, a lot."

"The body wanted him and you said no."

"I said wait."

"Because it wasn't your body."

"Because it wasn't my body."

"And if it was your body?"

I stop walking. Just stand on the sidewalk under a streetlight with the orange glow turning my shadow long and thin. "What?"

"If that arousal, that reaction, that wanting, if you felt that in your own body. What would you do?"

"That's, that's not the same question."

"I know. I'm asking it anyway."

I don't answer. Paco doesn't push. He waits. The television on his end changes to a commercial, someone selling car insurance with too much enthusiasm.

"I think the training is working," I say instead. "I think I'm getting better at knowing the difference."

"Between what the body wants and what you choose."

"Yeah."

"Good." He pauses. "Listen, the Brazilian. She sent me another gym video. She's doing deadlifts now. Her form is perfect, Manuel. Perfect. I'm going to fly to São Paulo and propose in the squat rack."

"You've never met her."

"Love doesn't require geography." He's grinning, I can hear it. "Also she asked me for a photo and I sent the one from your birthday party last year where I'm wearing the Hawaiian shirt and she said I looked 'simpático' which I THINK is a compliment?"

"It means nice."

"Nice! She thinks I'm nice!" The enthusiasm is back to full volume. The serious moment has passed. Paco lets serious moments pass the way a surfer lets waves pass, riding them just long enough and then letting the water take them.

"Get some sleep, hermano. Day 5 tomorrow. New body?"

"New body."

"What's this one called?"

"Renata."

"Renata." He says it like he's tasting it. "That sounds like a woman who doesn't take shit from anyone."

"I haven't met her yet."

"You'll tell me. You always tell me."

"Buenas noches, Paco."

"Buenas noches, Manuel. Try not to think about the knee."

He hangs up. I pocket the phone and walk the last four blocks home with the cold air on my face and the orange streetlights painting the sidewalk in strips and the echo of Katya's arousal still sitting in my lower belly like a coal that won't go out.
