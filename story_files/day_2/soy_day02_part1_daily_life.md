## PASSAGE: t0_day02_wake [day-start]

The alarm doesn't wake me. Chipi doesn't wake me. I'm already awake at 5:51, staring at the ceiling crack that looks like Baja, thinking about what it feels like to be pulled through something impossibly narrow.

Sixteen hours ago Rafael said it. "In approximately sixteen hours you're going to sit in a chair and someone is going to put your consciousness into the body of a woman." The hours ran out while I slept. Or didn't sleep. The night was a series of half-dreams where I kept reaching for things with hands that weren't mine, and every time I looked down they changed shape.

The alarm goes off at 6:15. I kill it. Chipi, who was sleeping on the pillow next to my head like a furry surveillance unit, opens one eye, decides I'm not interesting, and closes it again.

Kitchen. Coffee. The routine helps. Grind, filter, kettle, pour. My hands shake slightly when I hold the mug but the coffee is hot and good and I drink it standing at the counter in boxer shorts the same way I drank it yesterday and the day before and every day since I moved into this apartment. The sameness of it is a small anchor.

Toast. Butter. Chipi knocks the butter knife off the counter with one deliberate paw, watches it clatter to the floor, then looks at me with the expression of an animal who has never once in her life been responsible for anything.

"Really."

She blinks. Slowly. The blink of the innocent.

I pick up the knife. Wash it. Butter the toast. Eat it over the sink. The handbook says fasting is required before a transfer, nothing but water for four hours prior. My slot is afternoon, so morning food is fine. But the toast sits in my stomach like a brick. Nerves have a way of turning everything to concrete.

I shower. The water is hot and I stand under it too long again and Chipi sits on the toilet lid again and the mirror is fogged when I get out. I wipe it with my palm. Same face. Same body. Brown skin, dark hair curling over my ears, circles under my eyes darker than yesterday. The navy henley is in the hamper. I pull out a grey t-shirt and the same dark jeans. Boots.

I look at myself. This body. These shoulders, this flat chest, these hands with their knuckles and their veins and their specific way of gripping things. In a few hours I'm going to leave all of it behind, temporarily, and step into something completely different. Something with breasts and narrower shoulders and hips that change the way gravity works. Then I'm going to come back to this, to these hands and this chest and this jaw, and it's supposed to feel normal.

My leg starts bouncing. I grab my keys, my phone, my badge with the green stripe. Kiss Chipi on the head. She permits it.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_lulu_morning

The bells on the door. Lulu is restocking canned goods on the top shelf, standing on a step stool that makes her approximately the same height as me. She turns. Sees me. Sees my face.

"Mijo. Two days in a row? Either the science is very good or very bad."

"Just picking up breakfast."

"You already ate toast. I can tell. You have toast face." She steps off the stool. "Toast is not breakfast. Toast is a suggestion of breakfast. Here."

She reaches behind the counter. Wax-paper bag. "Empanadas. Same as yesterday. Beef and cheese."

"Lulu, I need to fast after eleven."

"It's seven-thirty. Eat now. Fast later." She pushes the bag across the counter. "Is the science making you fast? What kind of science makes a person not eat?"

"The medical kind."

Her eyes narrow. The crucifix gets a touch. "Medical. You said research yesterday."

"Medical research."

"Are they experimenting on you?" Her voice drops. Genuine concern underneath the interrogation. "Because I saw a show about that. Companies that experiment on immigrants because they think nobody will complain. Mijo, if someone is experimenting on you..."

"Nobody is experimenting on me. It's legitimate. It's safe. I promise."

She stares at me the way she stared at me yesterday, that long searching look, but today there's something sharper in it. Worry, maybe. The specific worry of a fifty-five-year-old bodega owner who's adopted a stray Mexican kid as her unofficial responsibility.

"You eat those empanadas before eleven. All of them."

"Yes, Lulu."

"God watches, Manuel."

"You said that yesterday."

"God is consistent. You should try it."

I eat one empanada on the walk to the bus. Beef and cheese. Perfect. Greasy. The cheese burns my mouth. Some things don't change.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_bus

The 34 bus. Same smell, diesel and breakfast sandwiches and metal. Different seat today, second row from the back, because the middle seats were taken by a group of high school kids with backpacks the size of body bags.

My leg bounces. I press my knee down. It bounces anyway. The kid next to me glances at my leg, then at his phone, then back at my leg. He's maybe sixteen, wearing earbuds that leak a tinny bass line. He doesn't say anything. Good.

The bus lurches through a yellow light. I grab the rail. My hand wraps around the metal and I'm conscious of the grip in a way I wasn't yesterday. These knuckles. This palm. The calluses on my fingertips from four years of typing at Brevity Labs, four years of code flowing through these specific fingers. In three hours these fingers will be different. Smaller. Smoother. Moles instead of calluses.

My phone vibrates. Mamá, sending a photo to the family group chat. Tía Rosa's new dog, small and white and angry-looking. My mother has typed "¡QUE LINDO!" with seven exclamation marks. Héctor has responded with a single thumbs up, which in Héctor-language is equivalent to a four-paragraph love letter.

I close the chat. Open the handbook instead. Chapter 3, section on pre-transfer anxiety management.

"*Anxiety prior to first transfer is universal and does not correlate with transfer success. Candidates who report high pre-transfer anxiety frequently outperform candidates who report confidence. The reason: anxiety indicates accurate risk assessment. Confidence, in a candidate who has never transferred, indicates either insufficient understanding or psychological suppression. Neither is useful.*"

Great. My anxiety is a feature, not a bug. Very reassuring.

Outside, October continues its slow demolition of the city's trees. The maples on Seventh Street are half-bare, their leaves scattered across sidewalks in orange and brown. A woman at the bus stop is adjusting her bra strap through her shirt, that quick two-finger pull at the shoulder, and I notice it the way I've started noticing things since yesterday's lecture. The mechanics of a body I'm about to inhabit. The small negotiations women make with their own clothing every ten minutes that I never registered before.

She catches me looking. Not at her body. At the gesture. But she doesn't know that. She crosses her arms. Turns slightly away. The universal posture of a woman who's been stared at on public transit enough times that the defense is automatic.

I look away. Out the window. My face is hot. I wasn't looking at her the way she thinks I was looking at her, but the guilt is the same, and there's a new dimension to it now, because in a few hours I'm going to be in a body that gets looked at like that, and the looking will land differently from the inside.

Twenty-six days. The number is one less than yesterday. The math keeps working in one direction.

The bus turns onto Ninth Street. 814 appears in the window like a face I'm starting to recognize. I pull the cord.

Cold air. Breath cloud. The heavy door.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_evening_walk

*(This passage fires after the transfer and debrief, when Manuel leaves the facility.)*

I walk.

Not to the bus. Past the bus stop, past Seventh Street, past the Dominican barbershop where the old men are gone for the evening and the chairs are folded and stacked against the wall. I walk because I need to feel my own body moving. My body. These legs, this stride, this weight pressing into the pavement with each step.

But the echoes are there.

My gait keeps trying to adjust. My hips want to swing wider than they should, compensating for a pelvis shape that isn't mine anymore. My center of gravity feels too high, like I'm walking on stilts when two hours ago I was walking six inches closer to the ground. I catch myself shortening my stride, then overcorrecting, taking too-long steps that feel wrong in the opposite direction.

My hands. I keep looking at my hands. They're too big. Not objectively too big, they're the same hands I've had for twenty-five years, but for two hours they were smaller and softer with short fingernails and a scattering of moles, and now they're these wide blunt instruments with knuckles and hair on the fingers and I can't stop turning them over and looking at them like they belong to someone else.

The cold helps. October air, sharp, biting through my grey t-shirt. My body's body temperature regulation kicks in immediately, goosebumps rising on my forearms, the hair standing up. In Flor's body the cold landed differently. Sharper somehow. More immediate. Like the skin itself was thinner.

I walk twelve blocks before I realize I'm heading to Danny's.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_dannys

The K-pop tonight is slower. Ballad territory. Danny's behind the counter slicing scallions with the precision of a man who takes green onions personally.

"Yo." He doesn't look up. "Spicy miso?"

"Spicy miso."

He glances at me. Does a double take. Sets the knife down.

"You look weird, man. Weirder than usual."

"Long day."

"Long day how? Like, long meeting long day? Or long existential crisis long day?"

"Somewhere in the middle."

He stares at me for another second. Danny has this thing where he looks at you like he's reading the menu that's written on your face, deciding what you actually need. Then he picks up the knife and goes back to the scallions.

"Extra chashu," he says. Not a question.

The bowl arrives. Red-gold broth, steam rising, the smell of miso and chili oil hitting my sinuses like a warm slap. I eat at the counter. Third stool from the left, the one with the wobble. The noodles are perfect. The chashu melts. The broth is hot enough to make my eyes water, or maybe that's not the broth.

I eat slowly. My body feels recalibrated after the walk but still not entirely mine. When I lift the chopsticks my fingers grip them the way my fingers have always gripped chopsticks, but I keep expecting them to be smaller. Keep expecting the chopsticks to feel proportionally different in a hand that isn't here anymore.

Danny slides a glass of water across the counter without being asked. "New job still going?"

"Still going."

"You eating enough? You look thinner."

"Everyone keeps telling me that."

"Because it's true." He wipes his hands on his apron. "Come back tomorrow. I'm testing a new tonkotsu batch. You're my guinea pig."

"I might be late."

"Broth doesn't care."

I finish the bowl. Drink the broth. Leave money. Danny pushes half back. I leave it as a tip. The dance continues.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_evening_home

The apartment. Chipi at the door, same position, same offended stare. I pick her up. She allows it for exactly three seconds before squirming free and jumping to the counter.

I feed her. Crack a Modelo. Stand at the counter and drink it and stare at the wall above the stove where there's a grease stain from the time I tried to make my mother's chiles rellenos and the oil popped and I flinched and dropped the whole pan.

The beer is cold. My apartment is warm. Chipi eats. The radiator ticks. Normal sounds. My sounds. Except everything is louder than it was this morning, or I'm more aware of it. My own breathing is deeper, coming from a wider chest. When I swallow the beer my throat feels thick. My adam's apple moves in a way I never noticed before because I never had a frame of reference for a throat without one.

I sit on the couch. Chipi jumps up. Settles on my chest.

Here's the thing. Three kilograms of cat on my chest. Warm, purring, her paws kneading the fabric of my t-shirt. I feel her weight on my flat chest and my brain sends a phantom signal, brief, barely there, expecting the pressure to land differently. Expecting something between Chipi's paws and my ribs that isn't there. Softness. Give. The slight redistribution of weight that happens when you press on a chest that has breasts.

The phantom passes. My chest is flat. My chest has always been flat. But for two hours it wasn't, and my nervous system hasn't finished updating the file.

I sit with Chipi on my chest and the beer in my hand and the apartment around me and I do nothing. I think about nothing. I watch the ceiling and the Baja Peninsula crack and I breathe.

In through the nose. My nose. My nostrils, which are wider than Flor's, which pull in more air per breath, which smell the apartment differently. Modelo. Cat food. The ghost of last night's rice and beans. Laundry I should do. The specific smell of a man who's been sweating through a consciousness transfer for two hours and then walking twelve blocks in October.

I should shower. I will shower. In a minute. For now I'm just going to sit here and remember what it feels like to be in this body, in this specific body, in this apartment, on this couch, with this cat, on this Tuesday night in October with twenty-six days left on a clock that might not matter anymore if I keep passing.

Chipi purrs. The radiator ticks. I close my eyes.

When I open them it's 9:47 PM. I fell asleep on the couch. Chipi has migrated to my lap. The Modelo is warm in my hand. My neck hurts from the couch angle. My body feels fully mine again, heavy and blunt and angular and mine.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_shower_night

The shower is different tonight.

Not the water, not the temperature, not the pressure. The shower itself is the same shower it's always been. What's different is me in it. I stand under the water and I'm aware of my body in a way I wasn't yesterday. The width of my shoulders. The flat plane of my chest where the water runs straight down without interruption, without the detour it took in Flor's body where it hit the curve of the breast and split into two streams. The way the water hits my hips, which are narrow, which don't flare, which are just bones and muscle and skin in a straight line from ribs to thigh.

My hand rests on my chest for a second. Flat. No weight. No give. Just pectoral muscle and rib and the steady thud of my heart underneath. Two hours ago this chest was different. Two hours ago this hand would have felt something there, something soft and warm and heavy enough to shift when I moved.

I take my hand off my chest. Finish the shower. Towel off. Stand in front of the fogged mirror and wipe it and look at myself. Same face. Same body. But the face knows something it didn't know yesterday. The body remembers a shape it tried on for two hours and can't quite forget.

I brush my teeth. Pull on boxers and the t-shirt I slept in last night. Crawl into bed. Chipi takes her spot at my hip.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_sleep

The bed is too big. Still too big. Left side, Chipi, unused pillow, ghost of a person I haven't met.

I lie on my back. Close my eyes. The day replays.

Elena's voice: "The thermal shift will feel like pressure behind your eyes. Don't fight it."

The heat. The compression. The narrow place. Then Flor's body, opening around me like a room I'd never been in.

Hands. Small hands. Short nails. Moles on the collarbones I could feel but not see.

The hip sway that happened without my permission. My name in a voice that wasn't mine.

The t-shirt exercise. Fabric catching on breasts. The weight of them settling when the shirt came off. Brief. Heavy. Real.

Then back. My body. Heavier. Louder. Blunter. Like stepping off a violin and back onto a drum.

My hand drifts to my chest again in the dark. Flat. The phantom is quieter now. Barely a whisper. But it's there. My nervous system filing a report it doesn't know how to label.

I don't act on anything. I'm not aroused. I'm not confused. I'm just aware. Aware of this body in a way I've never been before, because now I have exactly one other body to compare it to, and the comparison makes both of them specific instead of default.

Chipi purrs. I put my hand on her back instead of my chest. The vibration travels through my palm into my wrist. Small engine. Warm. Alive.

Twenty-six days. Tomorrow is Day 3. Tomorrow there's a new body. Tomorrow things get harder.

I fall asleep on the couch and the last thing I feel is Chipi's purr and the phantom weight of breasts I wore for two hours and the specific gravity of my own body pressing me into a mattress that's still too big for one person.

## PASSAGE: t0_day02_handbook_night

I pull out my phone. The Transfer Worker's Handbook. 340 pages. I managed three yesterday before Chipi fell asleep on my chest and my brain quit. Tonight I go further.

**Chapter 4: Post-Transfer Somatic Processing**

"*The first de-transfer is rarely the hardest. It is the hours following the first de-transfer that challenge most candidates. The body remembers shapes it has worn. Neural pathways activated during transfer do not immediately reset upon return to the natal body. Candidates may experience phantom weight distribution, gait adjustment echoes, proprioceptive confusion (reaching for objects with calibration appropriate to the transfer body rather than the natal body), and a general sense of unfamiliarity with their own physical form.*"

I stop reading. Put the phone on my chest. Chipi is curled at my hip, one paw resting on my thigh. The words on the screen are describing exactly what I've been feeling since 4 PM. The gait adjustment. The hand confusion. The flat chest that my nervous system keeps expecting to be different.

"*These sensations are normal and temporary. They typically resolve within twenty-four to forty-eight hours after a single transfer. With repeated transfers, the resolution period may shorten as the brain develops a more flexible somatic map. Alternatively, in a minority of candidates, the resolution period may extend, as the brain begins to maintain parallel somatic maps: one for the natal body, one for the most recent transfer body.*"

Parallel somatic maps. My body and Flor's body, existing simultaneously in my nervous system like two radio stations playing at once. Right now the signal is mostly mine, my station, my frequency. But the other station is there, faint, bleeding through. The phantom chest weight. The hip sway echo. The hands that feel too big.

"*Candidates experiencing persistent somatic echoes beyond forty-eight hours should report to their evaluator. Persistence is not pathological. It is data.*"

I read two more chapters. The words blur. My phone battery hits 12%. I plug it in, set it on the nightstand, and lie in the dark.

Chipi adjusts. Her paw lands on my stomach. Her purr is a low constant, a vibration I can feel through the mattress.

I put both hands flat on my own chest. Left hand. Right hand. Ten fingers spread across a flat ribcage with a heartbeat underneath. My heartbeat. My chest. My body. The one I was born in, the one I grew up in, the one that sat in forty-one interviews and ate Lulu's empanadas and slept in this too-big bed for three and a half years.

It feels specific tonight. Not default. Specific. Like one of several options rather than the only possibility. That's new. That's the thing I can't name. My body was always just my body, the water I swam in, invisible because it was everywhere. Now it's a glass of water sitting on a table. I can see the shape of it. I can see the edges.

I take my hands off my chest. Close my eyes.

Tomorrow there's a new body. Tomorrow the water changes shape again.

Chipi's purr carries me under. The last thought before sleep is the sensation of Flor's hands holding a pen, the letters coming out smaller than mine, the precision of fingers that traded strength for accuracy. Then the thought dissolves and I'm gone.
