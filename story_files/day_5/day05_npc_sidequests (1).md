## SQ-002: KEEPING APPEARANCES — LYLA'S FRIDAY INTERROGATION (FIRES)

### WINE BAR, 23RD STREET — 7:04 PM

She's already there. Of course she's already there. Lyla Chen does not arrive second to anything. She's at a corner table with a bottle of something red already open, two glasses already poured, and, I swear to God, a laminated piece of paper face-down on the table.

The wine bar is called Stave. Dim, wood-paneled, the kind of place that plays jazz at a volume designed to create the illusion of privacy without actually providing it. A couple at the bar is leaning into each other. A guy in the corner is reading a paperback with his wine untouched. The bartender is a tall man with a topknot and forearms covered in botanical tattoos.

Lyla sees me come in and her entire face lights up with predatory joy. Leather jacket, messy bun, the face of a woman who has been preparing for this conversation the way generals prepare for campaigns.

"Manuel González. Sit down. You're four minutes late and I have visual aids."

I sit. I pick up my wine. I drink before speaking because I need the half-second advantage.

"Lyla."

"Don't 'Lyla' me like that. Don't give me the soft eyes. You've been dodging me for five days and tonight I collect." She flips over the laminated page.

It's a chart. An actual chart. Handwritten in four colors of marker, with arrows, boxes, and a scoring system. The title, in red Sharpie: **WHAT DOES MANUEL ACTUALLY DO?**

Across the top: five theories. Down the side: evidence for, evidence against, and a probability score.

**Theory 1: Pharmaceutical test subject.** Evidence for: "biometric research" is vague enough. Evidence against: he'd have told me. Probability: 15%.

**Theory 2: Government spy.** Evidence for: visa desperation, secrecy, "classified" comment. Evidence against: he can't keep a poker face for more than twelve seconds. Probability: 5%.

**Theory 3: Underground fight club (nerd division).** Evidence for: "physical, learning how bodies work." Evidence against: have you SEEN his arms? Probability: 3%.

**Theory 4: Porn.** Evidence for: won't talk about it, involves bodies, pays well. Evidence against: he'd be WAY less stressed if it was porn. Probability: 8%.

**Theory 5: Something genuinely weird that he's embarrassed about.** Evidence for: ALL OF THE ABOVE. Evidence against: nothing, this is the correct answer. Probability: 69% (nice).

I stare at the chart. She stares at me. The jazz plays.

"You laminated this."

"I used the laminator at work. My terrible startup has exactly one good piece of equipment and I used it for this."

"The probability scores don't add up to 100."

"Math is a social construct, Manuel. TALK."

I drink more wine. It's good. Tempranillo, maybe. Earthy, warm. Like drinking a sunset through a screen door.

"What do you actually want to know?"

"Everything. Start with the basics. Company name?"

"I can't tell you."

"Location?"

"Downtown."

"Hours?"

"Eight to five. Roughly."

"What do you wear?"

"Regular clothes. They have a, work uniform for certain activities."

Her eyes narrow. "A work uniform. For certain activities. Manuel. This is not helping your case against Theory 4."

"It's not porn."

"A man in a work uniform doing physical activities with bodies, who can't tell his friends what the job is. Do you see why Theory 4 exists?"

"I see why Theory 4 exists. It's wrong."

"Then correct me."

I look at the wine. At the laminated chart. At Lyla, who flew to New York from San Francisco three years ago for a job she hates, who walked out of Brevity Labs holding a cardboard box the same day I did and said "well, that was dogshit" and became my friend in the space of one sentence. Lyla, who texts at 2 AM with memes and at 2 PM with job leads and who would walk into traffic for me and I for her and who is sitting across a table with a laminated chart because she cares enough to investigate.

**[PLAYER CHOICE: THE DISCLOSURE DECISION]**

This is the central choice of SQ-002. The consequences are significant and branching.

**Option A: THE DEFLECTION (Reveal nothing substantial)**

"It's a biotech company. They do research on neural interfaces. I'm a consultant who helps test the equipment. It involves being hooked up to machines, which is why it's physical. The NDA is real and comprehensive. I literally cannot tell you more without jeopardizing the visa pathway."

Lyla's reaction: skeptical but backed into a corner by the NDA argument. She can't push past a legal barrier without feeling guilty about potentially costing me the visa.

"An NDA. That's convenient."

"It's not convenient. It's contractual."

"You're hiding behind paperwork."

"I'm protecting my job. My job is protecting my visa. My visa is protecting my life here."

She goes quiet. Drinks wine. Her jaw works. She's angry but she's also a person who understands systems, and "NDA protects visa" is a system she can't argue with without becoming the villain.

"Fine." She refills both glasses. "I'm parking this. Not dropping it. Parking it. When the visa clears, and it WILL clear, I am going to sit you down with tequila, not wine, tequila, and you are going to tell me everything. Deal?"

"And if it turns out to be porn, I want residuals."

**Option B: THE PARTIAL TRUTH (Reveal the science, not the experience)**

"The company does consciousness transfer research."

Lyla puts her wine down.

"The technology exists to move a human consciousness from one body to another. Temporarily. Into lab-grown clone bodies. I'm testing the technology as a subject. They put my brain in a different body, I do tasks, they bring me back. The company classifies it as biometric research consulting and they can sponsor my visa."

Long silence. The jazz plays. The couple at the bar laughs at something.

"You're telling me your job is body-swapping."

"I'm telling you my job involves consciousness transfer into clone bodies for research and assessment purposes."

"That's the same sentence with bigger words." She leans forward. "What kind of bodies?"

Here it is. The door she's going to walk through.

"Female bodies."

Lyla's mouth opens. Closes. Opens again. Closes. She picks up her wine, drinks the entire glass in one pull, sets it down.

"You. Manuel González. Backend developer. The man who once spent twenty minutes explaining REST API architecture at a party. You are telling me that you, personally, put your brain into a woman's body."

"Female clone bodies. Yes."

"On PURPOSE."

"For work. For the visa."

"What does it feel like?"

"Different. Heavy, sometimes. Lighter, other times. The center of gravity shifts. Things, respond differently."

She leans back. Her eyes are enormous. The chart on the table suddenly looks like a children's drawing next to the reality.

"Oh my God." She presses her hands to her face. "Oh my GOD, Manuel. I had PORN. Porn was my fourth theory. Body-swapping wasn't even on the BOARD."

"In fairness, nobody would put body-swapping on the board."

"I need more wine." She pours. Drinks. Pours again. "Do you, and I need you to answer this, do you have BREASTS right now?"

"Right now? No. I'm in my own body right now."

"But you HAVE HAD breasts."

"During transfers, yes. The clone bodies are female. They have, yes. Breasts."

"What, what CUP SIZE?"

"Lyla."

"I'm asking for SCIENCE, Manuel."

"It's varied. By clone."

She puts her head on the table. The laminated chart crinkles under her forehead. She makes a sound that's halfway between laughter and screaming.

"I quit," she says into the table. "I quit being your friend. I'm going home. I'm going to lie in bed and think about the fact that my former coworker has had more experience with breasts this week than I've had in twenty-seven years and THEY'RE NOT EVEN HIS."

"Are you okay?"

She lifts her head. Her eyes are bright. Not with tears. With something else. Wonder, maybe. The specific wonder of a person whose worldview has just expanded by several dimensions.

"I'm incredible," she says. "I am having the best night of my entire life. Pour me more wine and tell me everything."

The conversation goes for another two hours. She asks about the science (I explain what I can). The training (I describe the modules in general terms). The ethical framework (she's fascinated). The legal structure (she has opinions). I don't tell her about the scenarios specifically, not the mirror, not the shower, not the partner, not the speculum. Those are mine. Those are inside things.

She doesn't push past what I offer. She's too smart to push someone who's finally talking.

At ten PM she puts both hands flat on the table.

"Manuel."

"Lyla."

"This is the most insane thing I've ever heard. You are doing the most insane job in the history of employment. And you're doing it for a VISA." She shakes her head. "I am, and I say this with complete sincerity, in awe."

"Don't be in awe. Be quiet about it. The NDA is real."

"I'm a vault. You know I'm a vault." She reaches across the table and takes my hand. Her grip is warm and firm and slightly drunk. "But Manuel. Are you okay? With all of this? The bodies and the, being a woman temporarily?"

"I'm processing."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the most honest one I have."

She squeezes my hand. Lets go. Picks up the laminated chart. Studies it.

"I need to update this." She pulls a marker from her jacket pocket because of course she brought a marker. She crosses out all five theories and writes, in large red letters across the bottom:

**THEORY 6: BODY SWAPPER. 100%. THE TRUTH IS ALWAYS WEIRDER.**

"I'm keeping this," she says, folding it carefully. "When they make a movie about your life, I'm going to sell this chart for millions."

*Consequences: Lyla relationship +2. Trust deepened significantly. She becomes a confidante. Future conversations reference the disclosure. She texts differently after this, more carefully, with awareness. She begins asking "how was work?" in a way that means something specific. She does NOT tell James. The secret is hers now. She takes it seriously. Over time, she becomes the person Manuel can process with outside the facility.*
